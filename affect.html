Affect: states that last

Everything this brain does so far, it does about the present. A bush is in front of it, so it bites. The skin is cold, so it walks. The stomach is empty, so it forages. When the cause is gone, the behaviour is gone in the same moment.

But some events say something about the minutes that follow. An animal that has just eaten well is probably standing in a good place. An animal that has just been hurt is probably standing in a bad one. To use that, the brain has to keep something of the event after it is over. This stage adds two such states: an alarm after pain, and contentment after a meal. So that the states have something to work with, the bushes in this world grow in patches that are mostly of one kind.

The circuit

Two modulators, substances that a few cells release and that reach many cells at once, and three new cells.

  • Alarm. Pain releases the modulator alarm. It drives a new cell, flee, which pushes the animal forward. After poison bites the animal keeps going for a while, out of the poisoned ground.
  • Serotonin. A new cell, fed, fires when the animal tastes food, and releases the modulator serotonin. It drives a new cell, settle, which switches on rest, the cell of the previous stage that holds the forward step back. After a good meal the animal stays where it ate, also where it is cold.

hungry still shuts rest, as before. So a fed animal stays on good ground until it is hungry again, and then it forages, with good bushes close by.

The two states are not a second system next to the drives. They act on the same cells: the drives decide what the animal does from what the body needs now, and the two modulators push those same modes for a while after an event.

States that build up

A modulator is released in small amounts and cleared slowly, so its level at any moment is a running sum of what happened lately, not a record of the last event alone. The cells that read it fire only above a threshold. One poison bite passes without alarm; the animal flees only when more than one comes within a short while. It settles only after more good bites than one bush can give.

That matters in this world, where a patch is mostly one kind but not only one kind. A single bite says little about the ground: a poison bush can stand in good ground, and a good bush in poor ground. Several bites close together say much more.

The wiring and the thresholds are designed. Evolution may tune every strength, the thresholds, and how fast each modulator is cleared, which sets how long each state lasts.

Watching it work

Below is one animal from the last generation at a poison bush.

It bites three times in a row. Each bite adds to alarm (the alarm row is full at the level where flee begins to fire). One bite is not enough; after the third, flee switches on, rest falls silent, and the animal walks forward every tick, away from the bush. The alarm is cleared slowly, so the flight outlasts the pain by more than ten ticks, and then flee falls silent again.

The biology: affect

A drive, as in the previous stage, follows the body: the hunger cell fires for as long as the stomach is empty and stops when it is full. An affective state is different in one respect. Something sets it off, and then it stays for a while by itself, after its cause has gone.

In small animals such states are carried by neuromodulators. They are released quickly and cleared slowly, and that slowness is the memory. The roundworm C. elegans slows down and stays where it is for minutes after it has found food, under the influence of serotonin. After a harmful touch it speeds away, and keeps going well after the touch has ended.

Such a state has two sides. It is good or bad, and it makes the animal more active or less. An animal that is alarmed is in a bad and active state. An animal that is content is in a good and quiet one.

The world: patches of mostly one kind

In the world of the previous stage the bushes of every kind were spread evenly. There, a meal says nothing about the place: what grows next to the bush just emptied is as likely to be poison as anywhere else. A state that lasts has nothing to work with.

So the bushes now grow in patches, and most bushes in a patch are of one kind. After good meals, the neighbouring bushes are most likely good as well. After poison bites, the neighbours are most likely poison too.

Not every bush in a patch is of the patch’s kind. In patches of only one kind, the first bite tells the animal all it needs to know about the bushes around it, and knowing a poison by its look, the lesson of the association stage, has nothing left to do. With other kinds standing among them, there is still a reason to know a bush by its look.

The world is also larger than before, four times the area for the same number of animals, with more food. In the smaller world the animals took food from each other, and the better they foraged the more they did, so that what a better forager gains was mostly hidden. This is the world the next chapter starts in.

The rest is as before: the world is cold, the hot springs are spread evenly, and the four kinds of berry bush are new in every life, half of them poison.

Results

Main against control

The control is the brain of the previous stage in this world of patches. It feels pain and tastes food as before, but nothing of either stays with it.

The animals with the two states come out ahead of the control in two seeds out of three. In the third the two end about level: re-evaluated in the same worlds the control is somewhat ahead, and when both live in one world together the main animals are slightly ahead. Over the three seeds the gain is clear when the two populations share a world, and weaker when each lives among its own kind.

Is the circuit used?

The two states are used in every seed. Silencing flee costs the most of the new cells: the alarm now acts on a run of poison bites, which in these mixed patches is the clearest sign of bad ground. fed and settle are two links of one chain and cost about the same. rest, through which both staying after a meal and staying in the warmth act, costs more than either.

Two things from earlier stages are not used in this world.

  • Silencing cold or warm_seek costs nothing, as with the first version of this stage. A fed animal that walks off in search of warmth leaves the good ground, and staying where the food is turns out to be worth more.
  • Switching the learning off costs nothing either. In these patches the look of a bush and the ground it stands on say much the same thing, and the animals lean on the ground: two poison bites in a row and the alarm carries them off. Learning is not lost, though. Its strength is still there in every seed, at about the level where a lesson stays on the food it was learned about. What keeps it from paying is that a lesson about one berry spreads to the berries that look like it. That is the problem the next chapter starts with.

Neither was removed. The cells belong to the previous stages as they were frozen, and the next stages need the learning.

After a bite

After poison bites the animals of this stage take a few more steps than the control, and after a good meal a little fewer. The differences are small in this measure, because one bite alone no longer sets a state off: it takes several.

How a life is spent

Both populations stand still for most of their life. Much of that time they bite at nothing: an animal held in place by rest still has its grasp programme, and a bite costs little.
